The best partner I worked with in the Azure channel turned down a deal in front of the customer.

The customer wanted a migration done in a timeline that couldn't hold. Two other partners had said yes. This one said, in the room, "we won't commit to that date, and here's what would have to be true for anyone to hit it." Then they listed the dependencies the customer hadn't mentioned.

They didn't win that engagement. They won the next three, because the customer had watched the first partner miss the date and blame the dependencies.

That taught me something I've carried into every role since. Saying no to the wrong version of the work is how you earn the right to do the right version. Agreeing to a timeline you don't believe in isn't customer focus. It's deferring the bad news to a point where it costs more.

The people who get trusted with the hard problems are the ones who were honest about the easy ones.

I try to be the partner who says no when it's warranted. It costs a deal now and then. It's cheaper than the alternative.

When did you last say no to a customer, and what happened?
