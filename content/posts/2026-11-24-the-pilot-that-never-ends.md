Most AI pilots don't fail. They just never end.

Six months in, the pilot is still running. It has users. It has a Teams channel. It has a budget line that gets renewed because cancelling it would mean admitting something. What it doesn't have is a decision.

The reason is almost always the same. Nobody wrote down, at the start, what "done" would look like. No threshold that would trigger a production build. No threshold that would trigger shutting it down. So the pilot lives in the space between, indefinitely.

When I scope one now, the exit criteria go in the first document, before the architecture. Something like: if accuracy on the fixed case set is above X and cost per run is below Y by this date, we build it properly, with this budget. If not, we stop, and here's what we'll have learned.

Both outcomes are wins. A pilot that ends with a clear "no" saved everyone a production build. A pilot that ends with a clear "yes" already has its business case written.

The only losing outcome is the one most pilots get: a slow drift into permanence without ever being designed to last.

Which of your pilots has a date on it?
