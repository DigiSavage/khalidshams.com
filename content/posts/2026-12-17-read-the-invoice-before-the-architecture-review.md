The cloud bill is the most honest architecture diagram an organisation has.

Diagrams show intent. The invoice shows what was actually built, what's still running, and what someone forgot to turn off. When I start an architecture review now, I ask for twelve months of cost data before I ask for a single slide.

It tells me things no interview does. The "decommissioned" environment that still costs four figures a month. The storage account growing at a rate nobody planned for. The premium tier bought during an incident and never revisited. The region nobody remembers choosing.

None of that is a cost problem, not really. It's an ownership problem that shows up as money. Every line on that bill without a name next to it is a decision nobody is making.

So the first deliverable in most of my reviews isn't a target architecture. It's the current bill, annotated: what each line is, who owns it, and whether anyone would notice if it stopped.

The target architecture is much easier to draw once that page exists. Half of it is already decided by what you're willing to keep paying for.

Have you read your bill line by line this year?
