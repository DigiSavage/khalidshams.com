People assume a solutions architect draws diagrams. Diagrams are maybe a tenth of it.

The job, as I've come to understand it over fifteen years, is owning the call. Standing between the people who pay for the system and the people who build it, and being accountable to both for a decision neither of them can fully make alone.

The business side knows what they need and what they can spend. They don't know what's possible or what it costs to make it hold up. The engineering side knows what's possible. They don't always know which of the possible things matters, or what the organisation will actually operate.

The architect's job is to turn that into a decision someone can act on. Not a menu of options. A recommendation, with the trade-offs stated plainly, and a name next to it.

That means being technical enough to be trusted by engineers and clear enough to be trusted by executives. It means saying "not in that timeline" out loud. It means being in the room when the promise is made, so you can be accountable when it's delivered.

The diagram is just the receipt.
