The platform should be dull. The product on top of it should be interesting.

Most teams get that backwards. The platform becomes the place where clever things happen: a custom orchestrator, a novel deployment scheme, a hand-rolled version of something the cloud already sells. Meanwhile the product, the thing customers actually see, gets whatever time is left.

I've been on both sides of this. Fifteen years in, my strong preference is for infrastructure that could be explained to a new engineer in an afternoon.

Managed services over self-hosted, unless there's a measured reason.
One deployment path, used by everyone, for everything.
Explicit ownership for every layer, written down.
Foundations built slightly heavier than today needs, so tomorrow doesn't require a rebuild.

None of that gets applause in a design review. All of it is why some systems are still running quietly five years later while others are on their second rewrite.

Boring is a feature. It's the feature that lets you spend your interesting on the things that matter.

Where is your platform being interesting when it should be boring?
