On the security and data strategy work I contributed to, the order of operations was the whole plan.

The temptation in any transformation is to start with the visible thing. Migrate the workload. Build the dashboard. Ship the AI feature. Then add the controls afterwards, once there's something to control.

It never works that way. Controls added afterwards are exceptions from day one. The workload already runs, the dashboard already has users, the feature already has a champion. Every control becomes a negotiation with something that's already in production.

So the sequencing rule was fixed early: identity and access boundaries land first. Then the data platform with ownership and policy in place. Then monitoring, wired to detections someone maintains. Then workloads, onto a foundation that already knows how to say no.

It looked slow on the Gantt chart. Every stakeholder wanted to see their thing earlier. What it produced was an estate where the AI features, when they arrived, inherited governance instead of fighting it.

Strategy documents talk about capabilities. The real strategy is which one you build first.

What did your programme build first, and what did that decide?
