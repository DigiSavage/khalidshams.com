In Azure customer success, consumption was the number everyone tracked. It was rarely the number that mattered.

A customer could be consuming plenty and still be in trouble. Workloads lifted but not landed. A platform team nobody had staffed. A migration that technically finished and practically nobody used. The consumption graph looked healthy right up until the renewal conversation.

The metric I ended up caring about was harder to put on a dashboard: how many workloads were running in production, owned by the customer's own team, without us on the call.

That one number captured everything. It meant the architecture was sound enough to run. It meant the handover had actually happened. It meant the customer had people who understood what they'd built. And it meant the next workload would be easier, because the first one had left a working pattern behind.

I still use it, in every engagement, in every role. Not "is it deployed" but "is it theirs." A system that only works when the person who built it is in the room isn't done. It's on loan.

What's the number you actually watch?
