I ran solution assessments at Microsoft for a stretch, across a lot of customers and a lot of estates.

The bad ones were easy to spot. They arrived at a recommendation that happened to be the product the vendor wanted to sell, and worked backwards to the evidence. Customers could smell it. Most of those assessments went into a drawer.

The good ones had a different shape. They told the customer at least one thing they didn't want to hear. Usually that the thing they were planning to buy wouldn't fix the problem they actually had, or that the problem was organisational and no licence would touch it.

The ones that changed anything had three properties.

They measured before they recommended. Real inventory, real usage, real dependencies, not the customer's own slide about themselves.
They separated findings from recommendations. Findings are facts. Recommendations are opinions. Mixing them is how trust gets lost.
They named an owner for every gap. A finding without an owner is a paragraph.

Thirty-two gaps and $4.5M in cost avoidance came out of one of those. Not because the assessment was clever. Because it was honest, and someone had to sign off on each line.

When did an assessment last tell you something you didn't want to hear?
