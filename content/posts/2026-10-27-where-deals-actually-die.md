Running an Azure partner channel taught me that deals rarely die in the sales meeting.

They die in the gap between the seller's slide and the delivery team's calendar.

The pattern was consistent. A partner would win on a strong proposal, then the engagement would stall for weeks because the people who scoped it were not the people who had to build it. Assumptions in the proposal met reality in the kickoff, and nobody had the authority to reconcile the two.

The partners who grew were not the ones with the best pitch. They were the ones where an architect sat in the sales conversation and could say, out loud, "we can do that, but not in that timeline, and here's what we'd cut."

That's uncomfortable in a sales cycle. It also builds the kind of trust that turns a project into a relationship.

I carry the same rule into my own work now. If I'm going to be accountable for delivering something, I want to be in the room when it's promised. Not to slow it down. To make sure what gets promised is the thing that can actually be built.

The most expensive sentence in enterprise technology is "we'll figure that out in delivery."

Who in your organisation is allowed to say "not in that timeline"?
