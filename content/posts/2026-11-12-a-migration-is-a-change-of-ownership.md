Customer success taught me that a migration isn't done at cutover.

It's done when the team on the other side can run the thing without calling you.

That sounds like a soft point. It's a hard one, because it changes what you plan for. If cutover is the finish line, you optimise for the weekend: rehearsals, rollback, the war room. Important, all of it. But teams who planned only for that weekend would call me a month later with the same three problems every time.

Nobody knew how to change it safely, because the change process lived with the migration team.
Costs had crept, because nobody on the receiving side owned the bill until it was already high.
Monitoring existed, but the alerts went to people who had rolled off.

So I started treating migration as a change of ownership, with cutover as one milestone inside it. The plan included who would own cost, who would own change, who would own the 3am page, and when each of those handovers happened. Not "after go-live." A date.

The migrations that stuck were the ones where the receiving team was bored by the end. Everything was theirs. Nothing was a surprise.

What did your last migration hand over besides the workload?
