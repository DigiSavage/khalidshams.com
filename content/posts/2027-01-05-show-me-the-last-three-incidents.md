The fastest way to understand a system isn't the architecture deck. It's the last three things that broke.

I ask for them in the first week of any engagement. Not the postmortems, if there are any. The raw story. What paged, who got woken up, what they tried first, what actually fixed it, and how long the whole thing took.

Three incidents tell you what the architecture deck can't.

Where the real dependencies are, because those are the things that failed together.
Who actually understands the system, because that's who got called at 2am.
What the team believes about the system that isn't true anymore, because that's why the first fix didn't work.
Which part of the design exists only on the slide, because that's the failover that didn't fail over.

Every organisation has a formal architecture and an operational one. The formal one is in the wiki. The operational one lives in the incident channel. The gap between them is where the risk sits, and it's also where the best improvements are cheapest.

Ask about the incidents first. The diagram will make more sense afterwards.

What did your last incident teach you that the documentation didn't?
