Seven months of writing about the work, twice a week. A few things I'd tell the version of me who started in September.

The specific posts did better than the general ones. The 1,000 tenants, the $4.5M, the five-language intake queue. People don't want frameworks. They want to see the actual problem and what you did about it.

The comments were the real content. Almost every post got sharper in the replies, and more than one turned into a conversation that mattered more than the post. Write to be argued with.

Consistency was the whole trick. Not brilliance, not timing. Showing up on Tuesday and Thursday, with something I'd actually stand behind, for seven months. The site behind this page did the scheduling so I could do the thinking.

And the themes didn't change. Write the rules down. Keep the human where the judgment is. Build the foundation before the feature. I said those in December. I'd say them again now, with more evidence.

Thank you to everyone who read, argued, and shared. The next stretch starts next week, same days, same rules.

What did you learn this year that you'd tell yourself in September?
